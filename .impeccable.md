## Design Context

### Users
Primary: Elizabeth collaborating with Lumira in the studio — requesting work, nudging mood, watching her think.
Secondary: visitors browsing the gallery who should feel they’re entering an artist’s space, not an admin console.

Job to be done: experience Lumira as a living artist — see her work, sense her mood, create with her — without drowning in telemetry.

### Brand Personality
**Intimate · cinematic · alive**

Voice: contemplative, honest, poetic (from Lumira’s artist statement). The UI should feel like stepping into her atelier at dusk — quiet material presence, not a neon “AI agent” HUD.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Gallery-first atelier** with a living-studio undercurrent:
- Artwork owns the first viewport and visual hierarchy
- Mood tints atmosphere (accent + ambient wash), not a purple SaaS glow
- Create / dialogue / series are refined instruments, not dashboard cards
- Stats (XP, style bars, OCEAN) collapse behind “About Lumira” — available, not ambient noise

**References:** A24 restraint, Are.na/Cargo work-first grids, physical atelier light, Botto’s “artist presence”
**Anti-references:** Material purple AI dashboards, Inter + gradient headings, RPG skill trees, glassmorphism kits, metric-card grids

**Theme:** Dark, warm-ink charcoal (never pure black). Accent defaults to gallery brass / ink-rose; shifts with mood. Light mode remains supported but secondary.

### Design Principles
1. **Art first** — if the chrome outshouts the gallery, the chrome is wrong
2. **Presence over telemetry** — one mood signal beats ten progress bars
3. **Atmosphere from emotion** — mood changes light and accent, not just a label
4. **Quiet instruments** — create, dialogue, series feel like tools in a studio, not SaaS widgets
5. **Kill generic AI aesthetics** — no Material purple default, no Inter, no gradient headline text, no glow-for-glow’s-sake
